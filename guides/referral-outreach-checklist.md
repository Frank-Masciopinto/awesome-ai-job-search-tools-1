# Referral Outreach Checklist

Referrals raise interview odds significantly. Most candidates do this badly — they send the same template to 30 people and get nothing.

This checklist gets you the good version.

## Step 1 — Pick the right role first

- [ ] You've read the JD twice.
- [ ] Your resume is tailored (or close enough).
- [ ] You'd actually take the job.

If you wouldn't take it, don't ask for a referral.

## Step 2 — Find the right person

Best to worst:

1. Someone you already know on that team.
2. An alum from your school on the team or adjacent.
3. An IC (engineer / DS / analyst) on the team.
4. A skip-level manager.
5. A recruiter (only if you can't find anyone else — they get spammed).

Avoid:

- The CEO.
- The VP of Engineering (unless it's a 30-person company).
- People who joined yesterday.
- People with `#opentowork` banners — they're job searching themselves.

## Step 3 — Find the contact

- LinkedIn DM is fine if you have Premium or a mutual connection.
- Work email via an [Email Finder](https://www.tryapplynow.com/tools/email-finder) is fine if your message is high quality.
- Don't cold-call. Don't message on weekends. Don't message at 2am.

## Step 4 — Write a good first message

Good first messages:

- Are under 120 words.
- Personalize the first line (something you saw on their LinkedIn, a project they shipped, a talk they gave).
- Name the specific role and link.
- Include one specific reason you'd be a fit (one project, one metric).
- Have ONE ask.
- End with "no pressure either way."

Bad first messages:

- "Hi Mr. [LastName], I hope this email finds you well."
- 400 words of life story.
- Asks for both a referral AND a coffee chat AND a resume review.
- Attached resume + cover letter + portfolio link + GitHub + Medium + Substack.

## Step 5 — One follow-up only

- Wait 5-7 business days.
- Send a 2-sentence bump: "Floating this back up. Totally fine if now isn't a good time."
- Stop after one follow-up. Don't be the third-time-asking person.

## Step 6 — Thank them

- If they referred you: thank them within 24 hours. Update them when you hear back.
- If they couldn't help: thank them anyway. Keep the relationship for next time.
- Don't be transactional. Help them later if you can.

## What to never do

- Don't message 20 people at the same company in one day.
- Don't pretend you already know someone.
- Don't lie about your background.
- Don't demand a referral.
- Don't re-message someone who said no.
- Don't tag them publicly on LinkedIn asking for a referral.
